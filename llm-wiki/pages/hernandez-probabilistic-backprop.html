Probabilistic Backpropagation

Probabilistic Backpropagation for Scalable Learning of Bayesian Neural Networks — Hernández-Lobato & Adams (ICML 2015)

📄 Paper: arXiv:1502.05336
📁 Source: 2015_Hernandez-Lobato_Probabilistic_Backprop.pdf
🎯 Core Idea

Instead of sampling weights (as in Bayes by Backprop), propagate Gaussian distributions through the network analytically using moment-matching. This gives a deterministic, closed-form approximation that is much faster and lower variance than Monte Carlo VI.

Mathematical Formulation

Assumed Density Filtering (ADF)

Assume each neuron's activation follows a Gaussian. For each layer, compute the mean and variance of the output exactly given Gaussian inputs and Gaussian weights:

$$ h = f(Wx + b), \quad W \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_W, \sigma_W^2),\; x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_x, \sigma_x^2) $$ $$ \mathbb{E}[h] = \mathbb{E}_W\!\big[\,\mathbb{E}_x[\,f(Wx + b) \mid W\,]\,\big] $$ $$ \mathrm{Var}[h] = \mathrm{Var}_W\!\big[\,\mathbb{E}_x[\,f(Wx + b) \mid W\,]\,\big] + \mathbb{E}_W\!\big[\,\mathrm{Var}_x[\,f(Wx + b) \mid W\,]\,\big] $$

For the linear part (pre-activation), the propagation is exact:

$$ \mathbb{E}[a] = \mu_W \mu_x + \mu_b $$ $$ \mathrm{Var}[a] = \mu_W^2 \sigma_x^2 + \sigma_W^2 \mu_x^2 + \sigma_W^2 \sigma_x^2 + \sigma_b^2 $$

Handling Nonlinearities

For activation functions like ReLU, tanh, or sigmoid, the moments are approximated via:

  • ReLU: Closed-form using error functions (assuming Gaussian input).
  • Tanh/sigmoid: Numerical quadrature or analytical approximations.

This is equivalent to assumed density filtering — approximate the true distribution with a Gaussian after each nonlinear transformation.

Algorithm

  1. Forward pass: Propagate mean and variance through each layer deterministically.
  2. Likelihood: Compute the Gaussian predictive distribution at the output.
  3. Update: Use a local variational approximation to update the weight posteriors (similar to expectation propagation).

Pros & Cons

✅ Advantages❌ Disadvantages
No Monte Carlo sampling — deterministic and fast Gaussian assumption after each nonlinearity is an approximation
Much lower gradient variance than Bayes by Backprop Moment-matching can be inaccurate for deep networks (error compounds)
Simple implementation for standard activation functions Limited to element-wise activations — hard for pooling, attention, etc.
Works well on small-to-medium datasets Less flexible than sampling-based methods for complex posteriors

Relevance Today

Probabilistic Backpropagation was a significant step forward in 2015, showing that BNNs could be trained deterministically. However, it has been largely superseded by:

  • Bayes by Backprop + improved variance reduction (more flexible, easier to implement generically).
  • Laplace approximation (post-hoc, works with any architecture).
  • Last-layer methods that avoid weight-space posterior altogether.

Still, the core technique of moment-matching is useful and appears in modern work on uncertainty propagation (e.g., Bayesian signal processing, probabilistic numerical methods).
