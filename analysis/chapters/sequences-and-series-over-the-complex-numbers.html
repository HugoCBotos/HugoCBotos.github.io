Complex exponential

Now, we define the exponential of complex numbers.

Theorem: The sequence qn=(1+zn)nq_n = \left(1+\frac zn\right)^n is a Cauchy sequence. Its limit is denoted by eze^z.

Proof:

By the binomial theorem, (1+zn)n=1+z+∑k=2nzkk!(1−1n)⋯(1−k−1n).\left(1+\frac zn\right)^n =1+z+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{z^k}{k!} \left(1-\frac1n\right)\cdots\left(1-\frac{k-1}{n}\right). Take m>nm>n. For 2≤k≤n2\leq k\leq n, each factor in the coefficient of zkz^k satisfies 0≤1−jn≤1−jm(1≤j≤k−1).0\leq1-\frac jn\leq1-\frac jm \qquad (1\leq j\leq k-1). Multiplying these inequalities shows that the coefficient of zkz^k in (1+zm)m\left(1+\frac zm\right)^m is at least as large as its coefficient in (1+zn)n\left(1+\frac zn\right)^n. The terms of degrees n+1,…,mn+1,\ldots,m occur only in the first polynomial and also have positive coefficients. The constant and linear terms cancel.

Thus, we can write qm−qn=b2z2+⋯+bmzm,q_m-q_n=b_2z^2+\cdots+b_mz^m, where every bk≥0b_k\geq0. By the triangle inequality, |qm−qn|≤b2|z|2+⋯+bm|z|m.|q_m-q_n|\leq b_2|z|^2+\cdots+b_m|z|^m. The sum on the right is exactly the same polynomial difference evaluated at |z||z| instead of zz. Therefore, |qm−qn|≤(1+|z|m)m−(1+|z|n)n.|q_m-q_n| \leq\left(1+\frac{|z|}{m}\right)^m -\left(1+\frac{|z|}{n}\right)^n. We already know that the real sequence (1+|z|n)n\left(1+\frac{|z|}{n}\right)^n converges to e|z|e^{|z|}. Consequently, the right-hand side tends to zero as n,m→∞n,m\to\infty. The case n>mn>m follows by interchanging the indices. Hence qnq_n is a Cauchy sequence. QED

Note that ez¯=ez¯\overline{e^z} = e^{\overline z}. Indeed, (1+zn)n¯=(1+z¯n)n,\overline{\left(1+\frac zn\right)^n} =\left(1+\frac{\overline z}{n}\right)^n, and taking the limit gives the identity.

Proof:

Let an=(1+zn)(1+wn),bn=1+z+wn.a_n=\left(1+\frac zn\right)\left(1+\frac wn\right), \qquad b_n=1+\frac{z+w}{n}. Then an−bn=zw/n2a_n-b_n=zw/n^2. Let R=|z|+|w|+|zw|R=|z|+|w|+|zw|. Euler Formula

To understand the complex exponential, it remains to find eiθe^{i\theta} for real θ\theta. We will show that eiθ=cos⁡(θ)+isin⁡(θ),e^{i \theta} = \cos(\theta) + i \sin(\theta), This is Euler's formula: eiθe^{i\theta} is the point on the unit circle obtained by rotating 11 through θ\theta radians.

Let us define f(θ)=cos⁡(θ)+isin⁡(θ)f(\theta) = \cos(\theta)+i \sin(\theta). First observe that f(α+β)=f(α)f(β),f(\alpha+\beta) = f(\alpha)f(\beta), because (cos⁡α+isin⁡α)(cos⁡β+isin⁡β)=(cos⁡αcos⁡β−sin⁡αsin⁡β)+i(cos⁡αsin⁡β+sin⁡αcos⁡β)=cos⁡(α+β)+isin⁡(α+β).\begin{align} (\cos\alpha+i\sin\alpha)(\cos\beta+i\sin\beta) &=(\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta)+i(\cos\alpha\sin\beta+\sin\alpha\cos\beta)\\ &=\cos(\alpha+\beta)+i\sin(\alpha+\beta). \end{align} Additionally, f(0)=1f(0) = 1 and |f(θ)|=1|f(\theta)| = 1 for any θ\theta.

Theorem: eiθ=cos⁡(θ)+isin⁡(θ)e^{i\theta} = \cos(\theta) + i \sin(\theta)

Proof:

For 0<θ<π/20<\theta<\pi/2, the blue region below is the difference between the sector and the triangle. Its area is (θ−sin⁡θ)/2(\theta-\sin\theta)/2. It lies inside the rectangle of width 1−cos⁡θ1-\cos\theta and height sin⁡θ\sin\theta.

Comparing areas, 0≤θ−sin⁡θ2≤(1−cos⁡θ)sin⁡θ≤1−cos⁡θ.0\leq\frac{\theta-\sin\theta}{2}\leq(1-\cos\theta)\sin\theta\leq1-\cos\theta. We already know that |sin⁡x|≤|x||\sin x|\leq|x|. Hence 0≤1−cos⁡θ=2sin⁡2(θ/2)≤θ22,0\leq1-\cos\theta=2\sin^2(\theta/2)\leq\frac{\theta^2}{2}, and consequently 0≤θ−sin⁡θ≤θ2.0\leq\theta-\sin\theta\leq\theta^2. For −π/2<θ<0-\pi/2<\theta<0, apply these bounds to −θ-\theta. Since sin⁡(−θ)=−sin⁡θ\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta and cos⁡(−θ)=cos⁡θ\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta, |sin⁡θ−θ|=|sin⁡(−θ)−(−θ)|≤θ2,|1−cos⁡θ|=|1−cos⁡(−θ)|≤θ22.\begin{align} |\sin\theta-\theta|&=|\sin(-\theta)-(-\theta)|\leq\theta^2,\\ |1-\cos\theta|&=|1-\cos(-\theta)|\leq\frac{\theta^2}{2}. \end{align} Both differences are zero at θ=0\theta=0. Thus, for |θ|<π/2|\theta|<\pi/2, |f(θ)−(1+iθ)|≤|cos⁡θ−1|+|sin⁡θ−θ|≤32θ2.\begin{align} |f(\theta)-(1+i\theta)| &\leq|\cos\theta-1|+|\sin\theta-\theta|\\ &\leq\frac32\theta^2. \end{align}

Now fix any real θ\theta. Since f(θ)=f(θ/n)nf(\theta)=f(\theta/n)^n, we compare f(θ/n)nf(\theta/n)^n with (1+iθ/n)n(1+i\theta/n)^n using an−bn=(a−b)(an−1+an−2b+⋯+bn−1).a^n-b^n=(a-b)(a^{n-1}+a^{n-2}b+\cdots+b^{n-1}). Take a=f(θ/n)a=f(\theta/n) and b=1+iθ/nb=1+i\theta/n. Since |a|=1|a|=1 and |b|≤1+|θ|/n|b|\leq1+|\theta|/n, each of the nn terms in parentheses has absolute value at most (1+|θ|/n)n−1(1+|\theta|/n)^{n-1}. The triangle inequality then gives, for sufficiently large nn, |f(θ)−(1+iθn)n|≤n|f(θ/n)−(1+iθn)|(1+|θ|n)n−1≤3θ22n(1+|θ|n)n−1→0.\begin{align} \left|f(\theta)-\left(1+\frac{i\theta}{n}\right)^n\right| &\leq n\left|f(\theta/n)-\left(1+\frac{i\theta}{n}\right)\right|\left(1+\frac{|\theta|}{n}\right)^{n-1}\\ &\leq\frac{3\theta^2}{2n}\left(1+\frac{|\theta|}{n}\right)^{n-1}\to0. \end{align} The last factor tends to e|θ|e^{|\theta|}. Therefore, eiθ=f(θ)=cos⁡θ+isin⁡θ.e^{i\theta}=f(\theta)=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta. QED
