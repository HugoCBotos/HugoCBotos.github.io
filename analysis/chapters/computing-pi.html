Inscribed polygons: approaching \(\pi\) from below

Consider a regular \(2^n\)-agon inscribed in the unit circle, with \(n\geq 2\). Each vertex lies on the circle. Joining the center to the vertices divides the polygon into \(2^n\) congruent isosceles triangles. The highlighted triangle is one of these pieces.

Each triangle has two sides of length \(1\) and included angle \(2\pi/2^n\). Using the formula \(\frac12 ab\sin\theta\) for the area of a triangle, its area is \[\frac12\cdot 1\cdot 1\cdot\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{2^n}\right) =\frac12\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{2^n}\right).\] Therefore, the area of the inscribed polygon is \[A_n=2^{n-1}\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{2^n}\right).\]

Since \(\sin(\pi/2)=1\), we start with \(A_2=2\), the area of the inscribed square. Doubling the number of sides halves the central angle.

Theorem: For \(n\geq 2\) we have the recursion: \[A_{n+1}=2^n\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\dfrac{A_n^2}{2^{2n-2}}}}{2}}, \qquad n\geq 2.\]

Proof:

To compute the next area from the preceding one, use the half-angle identity \[\sin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{1-\cos x}{2}}.\] For \(0\leq x\leq\pi/2\), we have \(\cos x\geq 0\), so \(\cos x=\sqrt{1-\sin^2x}\). Thus \[\sin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) =\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\sin^2x}}{2}}, \qquad 0\leq x\leq\frac{\pi}{2}.\] Substituting \(x=2\pi/2^n\) and \(\sin(2\pi/2^n)=A_n/2^{n-1}\) gives \[\frac{A_{n+1}}{2^n} =\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\dfrac{A_n^2}{2^{2n-2}}}}{2}}.\] Multiplying by \(2^n\), we obtain the recurrence \[A_{n+1}=2^n\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\dfrac{A_n^2}{2^{2n-2}}}}{2}}, \qquad n\geq 2.\] QED

Starting from \(A_2=2\), we can compute \(A_3\), then \(A_4\), and so on, using only arithmetic and square roots. This gives the following table.

Its area increases toward \(\pi\), the area of the unit circle, while remaining strictly below it: \[A_n<\pi.\] For \(n=12\), the polygon has \(2^{12}=4096\) sides and area approximately \(3.1415914215\).

Circumscribed polygons: approaching \(\pi\) from above

Now consider a regular \(2^n\)-agon circumscribed about the unit circle, with \(n\geq 2\). Each side is tangent to the circle. Joining the center to the vertices divides the polygon into \(2^n\) congruent isosceles triangles. The highlighted triangle is one of these pieces.

The altitude of each triangle is a radius of the circle, so it has length \(1\). This altitude bisects both the base and the central angle \(2\pi/2^n\). Thus half the base has length \(\tan(\pi/2^n)\), and the area of one triangle is \[\frac12\cdot 2\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2^n}\right)\cdot 1 =\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2^n}\right).\] Therefore, the area of the circumscribed polygon is \[B_n=2^n\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2^n}\right).\]

Theorem : For \(n\geq 2\) we have the recursion: \[B_{n+1}=\frac{2B_n}{1+\sqrt{1+\dfrac{B_n^2}{2^{2n}}}}, \qquad n\geq 2.\]

Proof:

Since \(\tan(\pi/4)=1\), we start with \(B_2=4\). To compute the next area from the preceding one, use \[\tan\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) =\frac{\sin x}{1+\cos x} =\frac{\tan x}{1+\sqrt{1+\tan^2x}}, \qquad 0\leq x<\frac{\pi}{2}.\] Substituting \(x=\pi/2^n\) and \(\tan(\pi/2^n)=B_n/2^n\) gives \[B_{n+1}=\frac{2B_n}{1+\sqrt{1+\dfrac{B_n^2}{2^{2n}}}}, \qquad n\geq 2.\] QED

The circumscribed areas decrease toward \(\pi\), while the inscribed areas increase toward \(\pi\). Because each inscribed polygon lies inside the circle and each circumscribed polygon contains it, we have \[A_n<\pi<B_n.\] For \(n=12\), both polygons have \(4096\) sides. Their areas give the bounds \[3.1415914215<\pi<3.1415932697.\] The displayed lower bound is rounded down and the upper bound is rounded up.

Estimating the error

The inscribed areas \(A_n\) increase, the circumscribed areas \(B_n\) decrease, and the circle lies between the two polygons: \[A_n\leq\pi\leq B_n.\] To prove that both sequences converge to \(\pi\), we will estimate the gap \(B_n-A_n\). We first need a geometric inequality.

Theorem. For every real number \(x\) with \(|x|<\pi\), \[|\sin x|\leq |x|.\]

Proof.

The case \(x=0\) is immediate. Otherwise, \(0<|x|<\pi\). In the unit circle, consider the triangle with vertices \[O=(0,0),\qquad P=(1,0),\qquad Q=(\cos|x|,\sin|x|).\] The central angle \(POQ\) is \(|x|\), measured in radians.

The triangle has base \(OP=1\) and height \(\sin|x|\), so its area is \(\frac12\sin|x|\). The sector with radius \(1\) and angle \(|x|\) has area \(\frac12|x|\). Since the triangle lies inside the sector, \[\frac{\sin|x|}{2}\leq\frac{|x|}{2}.\] Multiplying by \(2\) and using \(\sin|x|=|\sin x|\) for \(|x|<\pi\), we obtain \[|\sin x|\leq |x|.\] QED

Theorem: For \(n\geq 2\) we have \[1-\frac{\pi^2}{2^{2n}}\leq\frac{A_n}{B_n}\leq 1.\]

Proof:

For \(n\geq 2\), write \(\theta_n=\pi/2^n\). The double-angle identity gives \[A_n=2^{n-1}\sin(2\theta_n) =2^n\sin\theta_n\cos\theta_n,\] while \[B_n=2^n\tan\theta_n =2^n\frac{\sin\theta_n}{\cos\theta_n}.\] Dividing, we obtain \[\frac{A_n}{B_n}=\cos^2\theta_n=1-\sin^2\theta_n.\] By the sine inequality, \(0\leq\sin^2\theta_n\leq\theta_n^2\). Therefore, \[1-\frac{\pi^2}{2^{2n}}\leq\frac{A_n}{B_n}\leq 1.\] QED

To estimate the gap between the two areas, multiply by \(B_n\): \[0\leq B_n-A_n =B_n\left(1-\frac{A_n}{B_n}\right) \leq B_n\frac{\pi^2}{2^{2n}}.\] The circumscribed polygons are nested inside the initial circumscribed square, so \(0<B_n\leq B_2=4\). Therefore, \[0\leq B_n-A_n\leq\frac{4\pi^2}{2^{2n}}.\] The right-hand side tends to \(0\), hence \(B_n-A_n\to 0\).

Finally, because \(A_n\leq\pi\leq B_n\), each approximation error is at most the gap between the polygon areas: \[0\leq\pi-A_n\leq B_n-A_n\leq\frac{4\pi^2}{2^{2n}},\] \[0\leq B_n-\pi\leq B_n-A_n\leq\frac{4\pi^2}{2^{2n}}.\] Both errors tend to \(0\). Thus \[A_n\to\pi\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_n\to\pi.\] Each time we double the number of sides, this upper bound for the error is divided by \(4\).
