Inscribed polygons: approaching π\pi from below

Consider a regular 2n2^n-agon inscribed in the unit circle, with n≥2n\geq 2. Each vertex lies on the circle. Joining the center to the vertices divides the polygon into 2n2^n congruent isosceles triangles. The highlighted triangle is one of these pieces.

Each triangle has two sides of length 11 and included angle 2π/2n2\pi/2^n. Using the formula 12absin⁡θ\frac12 ab\sin\theta for the area of a triangle, its area is 12⋅1⋅1⋅sin⁡(2π2n)=12sin⁡(2π2n).\frac12\cdot 1\cdot 1\cdot\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{2^n}\right) =\frac12\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{2^n}\right). Therefore, the area of the inscribed polygon is An=2n−1sin⁡(2π2n).A_n=2^{n-1}\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{2^n}\right).

Since sin⁡(π/2)=1\sin(\pi/2)=1, we start with A2=2A_2=2, the area of the inscribed square. Doubling the number of sides halves the central angle.

For 0≤x≤π/20\leq x\leq\pi/2, we have cos⁡x≥0\cos x\geq 0, so cos⁡x=1−sin⁡2x\cos x=\sqrt{1-\sin^2x}. Thus sin⁡(x2)=1−1−sin⁡2x2,0≤x≤π2.\sin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) =\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\sin^2x}}{2}}, \qquad 0\leq x\leq\frac{\pi}{2}. Circumscribed polygons: approaching π\pi from above

Now consider a regular 2n2^n-agon circumscribed about the unit circle, with n≥2n\geq 2. Each side is tangent to the circle. Joining the center to the vertices divides the polygon into 2n2^n congruent isosceles triangles. The highlighted triangle is one of these pieces.

The altitude of each triangle is a radius of the circle, so it has length 11. This altitude bisects both the base and the central angle 2π/2n2\pi/2^n. Thus half the base has length tan⁡(π/2n)\tan(\pi/2^n), and the area of one triangle is 12⋅2tan⁡(π2n)⋅1=tan⁡(π2n).\frac12\cdot 2\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2^n}\right)\cdot 1 =\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2^n}\right). Therefore, the area of the circumscribed polygon is Bn=2ntan⁡(π2n).B_n=2^n\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2^n}\right).

Theorem: For n≥2n\geq 2 we have the recursion: Bn+1=2Bn1+1+Bn222n,n≥2.B_{n+1}=\frac{2B_n}{1+\sqrt{1+\dfrac{B_n^2}{2^{2n}}}}, \qquad n\geq 2.

Proof:

Since tan⁡(π/4)=1\tan(\pi/4)=1, we start with B2=4B_2=4. To compute the next area from the preceding one, use tan⁡(x2)=sin⁡x1+cos⁡x=tan⁡x1+1+tan⁡2x,0≤x<π2.\tan\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) =\frac{\sin x}{1+\cos x} =\frac{\tan x}{1+\sqrt{1+\tan^2x}}, \qquad 0\leq x<\frac{\pi}{2}. Substituting x=π/2nx=\pi/2^n and tan⁡(π/2n)=Bn/2n\tan(\pi/2^n)=B_n/2^n gives Bn+1=2Bn1+1+Bn222n,n≥2.B_{n+1}=\frac{2B_n}{1+\sqrt{1+\dfrac{B_n^2}{2^{2n}}}}, \qquad n\geq 2. QED

Estimating the error

The inscribed areas AnA_n increase, the circumscribed areas BnB_n decrease, and the circle lies between the two polygons: An≤π≤Bn.A_n\leq\pi\leq B_n. To prove that both sequences converge to π\pi, we will estimate the gap Bn−AnB_n-A_n. We first need a geometric inequality.

Theorem. For every real number xx with |x|<π|x|<\pi, |sin⁡x|≤|x|.|\sin x|\leq |x|.

Proof.

The case x=0x=0 is immediate. Otherwise, 0<|x|<π0<|x|<\pi. In the unit circle, consider the triangle with vertices O=(0,0),P=(1,0),Q=(cos⁡|x|,sin⁡|x|).O=(0,0),\qquad P=(1,0),\qquad Q=(\cos|x|,\sin|x|). The central angle POQPOQ is |x||x|, measured in radians.

The triangle has base OP=1OP=1 and height sin⁡|x|\sin|x|, so its area is 12sin⁡|x|\frac12\sin|x|. The sector with radius 11 and angle |x||x| has area 12|x|\frac12|x|. Since the triangle lies inside the sector, sin⁡|x|2≤|x|2.\frac{\sin|x|}{2}\leq\frac{|x|}{2}. Multiplying by 22 and using sin⁡|x|=|sin⁡x|\sin|x|=|\sin x| for |x|<π|x|<\pi, we obtain |sin⁡x|≤|x|.|\sin x|\leq |x|. QED

Theorem: For n≥2n\geq 2 we have 1−π222n≤AnBn≤1.1-\frac{\pi^2}{2^{2n}}\leq\frac{A_n}{B_n}\leq 1.

Proof:

For n≥2n\geq 2, write θn=π/2n\theta_n=\pi/2^n. The double-angle identity gives An=2n−1sin⁡(2θn)=2nsin⁡θncos⁡θn,A_n=2^{n-1}\sin(2\theta_n) =2^n\sin\theta_n\cos\theta_n, while Bn=2ntan⁡θn=2nsin⁡θncos⁡θn.B_n=2^n\tan\theta_n =2^n\frac{\sin\theta_n}{\cos\theta_n}. Dividing, we obtain AnBn=cos⁡2θn=1−sin⁡2θn.\frac{A_n}{B_n}=\cos^2\theta_n=1-\sin^2\theta_n. By the sine inequality, 0≤sin⁡2θn≤θn20\leq\sin^2\theta_n\leq\theta_n^2. Therefore, 1−π222n≤AnBn≤1.1-\frac{\pi^2}{2^{2n}}\leq\frac{A_n}{B_n}\leq 1. QED
