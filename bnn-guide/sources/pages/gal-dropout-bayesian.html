Dropout as Bayesian Approximation

Dropout as a Bayesian Approximation: Representing Model Uncertainty in Deep Learning — Gal & Ghahramani (ICML 2016)

📄 Paper: arXiv:1506.02142
📁 Source: 2016_Gal_Dropout_Bayesian_Approximation.pdf
🎯 Core Idea

Standard dropout training (with dropout before every weight layer) is mathematically equivalent to variational inference in a deep Gaussian process. This means any neural network trained with dropout is already a Bayesian model — you just need to use Monte Carlo dropout at test time to extract uncertainty.

Mathematical Formulation

Dropout as Variational Inference

The key insight: dropout's stochastic binary mask can be interpreted as a variational distribution q(w) over the weights:

q(w) = w ⊙ diag(z),   z_i ~ Bernoulli(p)

where p is the keep probability. This is a mixture of a point mass at zero and the learned weight — a "multiplicative" Bernoulli noise on the weights.

Gal shows that optimizing the standard dropout objective (cross-entropy + L2 regularization) is equivalent to optimizing a valid ELBO, where:

L_dropout ≈ L_ELBO = E_{q(w)}[log p(D|w)] - KL[q(w) || p(w)]

The L2 regularization term corresponds to a Gaussian prior p(w). The dropout rate controls the KL divergence.

MC Dropout — Extracting Uncertainty

At test time, instead of turning off dropout, keep it on and perform T stochastic forward passes:

p(y*|x*, D) ≈ (1/T) Σ_{t=1}^T p(y*|x*, ŵ_t),   ŵ_t ~ q(w)

This gives you:

  • Predictive mean: average of the T outputs
  • Predictive uncertainty: variance of the T outputs

Practical Implementation

# Training: standard dropout (no change needed!)
model = nn.Sequential(
    nn.Linear(128, 256),
    nn.Dropout(0.5),
    nn.ReLU(),
    nn.Linear(256, 10)
)

# Testing: MC Dropout
model.train()  # keep dropout active!
predictions = []
for _ in range(T):
    pred = model(x)
    predictions.append(pred)
predictions = torch.stack(predictions)
mean = predictions.mean(dim=0)
variance = predictions.var(dim=0)

Pros & Cons

✅ Advantages❌ Disadvantages
Zero extra training cost — use any existing model The Bernoulli variational posterior is very restrictive
Trivial to implement (just keep dropout on at test time) Uncertainty quality depends on dropout rate (needs tuning)
Works for any architecture with dropout Not a "full" Bayesian treatment — no true distribution over weights
Provides calibrated uncertainty estimates in practice Overconfident far from training data (unlike true Gaussian processes)

Key Takeaways for Practitioners

  • You are probably already doing Bayesian deep learning if you use dropout. MC Dropout gives you uncertainty at near-zero extra training cost (though dropout may require more epochs to converge, so the effective cost is not exactly zero).
  • The quality of uncertainty depends on the dropout rate — higher rates give more conservative (wider) uncertainty but may hurt predictive accuracy.
  • Concrete dropout (Gal et al., 2017 extension) learns the dropout rate automatically via gradient-based optimization.
  • For production systems where uncertainty matters, MC Dropout is often the most practical first step before trying more expensive methods.
🔗 Connections: This paper bridges deterministic deep learning and Bayesian inference. It inspired later work on Bayesian approximations via other forms of stochastic regularization (e.g., DropConnect, Shake-Shake). Izmailov et al. (2021) benchmark MC Dropout against HMC and show it provides reasonable but not perfect uncertainty calibration.
