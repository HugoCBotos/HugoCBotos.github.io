Bayes by Backprop

Weight Uncertainty in Neural Networks — Blundell, Cornebise, Kavukcuoglu, Wierstra (2015)

📄 Paper: arXiv:1505.05424
📁 Source: 2015_Blundell_Bayes_by_Backprop.pdf
🎯 Core Idea

Learn a distribution over neural network weights by optimizing a variational lower bound using the reparameterization trick — enabling standard backpropagation on Bayesian neural networks.

Mathematical Formulation

Variational Objective

We want to approximate the true posterior p(w|D) with a variational distribution q(w|θ) (typically a Gaussian with mean μ and standard deviation σ). Minimizing KL[q(w|θ) || p(w|D)] is equivalent to maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO):

ELBO = E_{q(w|θ)}[log p(D|w)] - KL[q(w|θ) || p(w)]

where the first term is the data-likelihood (fitting the data) and the second term is a regularizer towards the prior.

Reparameterization Trick

The key enabler: instead of sampling w ~ N(μ, σ²) directly (which is non-differentiable), write:

w = μ + σ ⊙ ε,    where ε ~ N(0, I)

Now gradients can flow through μ and σ via the deterministic path.

Local Reparameterization

To reduce gradient variance, Blundell et al. sample the pre-activation (the weighted sum) rather than individual weights. For a layer with input a and weights w ~ N(μ, σ²):

b = wa  ⇒  b ~ N(μa, σ²a²)

This makes the ELBO gradients much lower variance and stabilizes training.

Algorithm Summary

  1. Forward pass: Sample weights from q(w|θ) via reparameterization.
  2. Compute loss: loss = log_likelihood + (1/N) * KL[q || p] (where N = number of mini-batches for unbiased estimation).
  3. Backward pass: Gradients w.r.t. μ and ρ (where σ = log(1 + exp(ρ))) flow through the sampled weights.
  4. Update: Standard optimizer (Adam, SGD) updates the variational parameters.

Practical Implementation Notes

  • Prior choice: Scale mixture of Gaussians (two Gaussians with different variances) works better than a single Gaussian — encourages both large and small weights.
  • Parameterization of σ: Use the softplus transformation: σ = log(1 + exp(ρ)) to ensure positivity and allow unconstrained optimization of ρ.
  • Complexity: ≈ 2× the parameters of a deterministic network (μ and ρ per weight). Forward pass is ≈ 2× slower (due to sampling).
  • KL annealing: Gradually increasing the KL weight during training often helps.

Pros & Cons for NN Practitioners

✅ Advantages❌ Disadvantages
Principled Bayesian inference with uncertainty estimates Gaussian posterior assumption is restrictive — real posteriors are often multi-modal and heavy-tailed
Works with any architecture (MLP, CNN, RNN) 2× parameter blowup can be significant for large models
Standard backpropagation — easy to implement in PyTorch/TF Gradient estimates can still be high-variance for deep networks
Provides both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty Diagonal covariance posterior ignores weight correlations
🔗 Connections: This paper is the foundation for most modern VI-based BNN work. Louizos & Welling (2017) extended it with normalizing flows for richer posteriors. Izmailov et al. (2021) showed that the Gaussian posterior assumption is often severely misspecified — consider this when interpreting uncertainty.
