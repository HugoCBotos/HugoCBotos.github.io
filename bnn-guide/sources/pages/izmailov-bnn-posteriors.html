What Are Bayesian Neural Network Posteriors Really Like?

Izmailov, Vikram, Cowen-Rivers, Foong, Padhy, Daxberger, Hu, Wilson, Dellaportas, Duvenaud — ICML 2021

📄 Paper: arXiv:2104.14421
📁 Source: 2021_Izmailov_BNN_Posteriors.pdf
🎯 Core Idea

A large-scale empirical study comparing HMC (gold standard) against variational inference, Laplace, SWAG, and MC Dropout. The key conclusion: all common approximations are significantly misspecified — true posteriors are heavy-tailed, multi-modal, and have complex correlation structure that simple Gaussian/mean-field assumptions cannot capture.

Critical Findings

1. Posteriors Are Not Gaussian

Using HMC as ground truth on small-to-medium architectures (ResNet-20, etc.), the study finds:

  • Heavy tails: Weight marginals have kurtosis much larger than 3 (Gaussian).
  • Multi-modality: Multiple distinct modes exist in weight space (symmetries, scaling degeneracies).
  • Complex correlations: Off-diagonal correlations between weights are strong and structured.

2. VI Is Overconfident

Mean-field Gaussian VI consistently underestimates uncertainty — the variational posterior is too narrow (typical of KL[q||p] minimization which penalizes q having mass where p has none).

3. Laplace Is Locally Accurate

The Laplace approximation is reasonable near the mode but fails to capture the global posterior structure. Last-layer Laplace performs surprisingly well on predictive metrics.

4. MC Dropout Is Surprisingly Robust

Despite its crude Bernoulli approximation, MC Dropout provides well-calibrated predictive uncertainty — often better than VI. However, it cannot provide weight-space uncertainty.

Implications for Research & Practice

MessageImpact
Be skeptical of uncertainty numbers All approximations are systematically wrong. Calibrate outputs whenever possible.
HMC is the reference, but expensive Use HMC for small-scale studies to understand what the posterior actually looks like.
Ensembles help The paper suggests that simple deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) often match or exceed dedicated BNN methods — possibly because they capture multi-modality better.
Focus on predictive (not weight) uncertainty For practical applications, predictive uncertainty is what matters, and the best method depends on the specific task and architecture.

Methods Compared

  • HMC: Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (gold standard, via NUTS)
  • VI: Mean-field Gaussian variational inference (Bayes by Backprop)
  • Laplace: Full, K-FAC, diagonal, last-layer
  • SWAG: Stochastic Weight Averaging Gaussian (Maddox et al., 2019)
  • MC Dropout: Monte Carlo dropout
  • Deep Ensembles: Multiple independent MAP trainings
⚠️ Takeaway: This paper is essential reading before committing to any BNN approximation. It fundamentally argues that the field's focus on improving variational bounds may be misplaced — the real solution likely requires richer posterior families (normalizing flows, neural transport, or particle-based methods).
