A Conceptual Introduction to Hamiltonian Monte Carlo

Michael Betancourt (2017, arXiv)

📄 Paper: arXiv:1701.02434
📁 Source: 2017_Betancourt_HMC_Introduction.pdf
🎯 Core Idea

HMC is the gold-standard MCMC method for Bayesian neural networks. It uses Hamiltonian dynamics (from physics) to propose distant states with high acceptance probability, making it far more efficient than random-walk Metropolis-Hastings in high dimensions. This tutorial explains the geometry and practical diagnostics.

Why HMC for BNNs?

BNN posteriors are:

  • High-dimensional (thousands to millions of parameters)
  • Strongly correlated (weights interact in complex ways)
  • Multi-modal (many equivalent solutions due to symmetries)

Random-walk Metropolis fails catastrophically in such spaces. HMC leverages gradient information to traverse the posterior efficiently.

Core Intuition: Analogy to Physics

Think of a frictionless puck sliding on a landscape:

  • Potential energy: $U(w) = -\log p(w \mid D)$ (the negative log-posterior)
  • Kinetic energy: $K(p) = \frac{1}{2} p^T M^{-1} p$ (momentum $p$, mass matrix $M$)
  • Hamiltonian: $H(w, p) = U(w) + K(p)$ — total energy conserved

HMC simulates the Hamiltonian dynamics using leapfrog integration. After each trajectory, the momentum is randomly resampled, and the proposed (w, p) is accepted/rejected based on the energy difference (which is near-zero with accurate integration, giving high acceptance).

Key Components in Practice

ComponentRolePractical Choice
Mass matrix MDefines the metric (shapes the proposal)Diagonal from empirical Fisher; adapt during warmup
Step size $\varepsilon$Integration accuracyAdapt to achieve ~0.65 acceptance rate (NUTS)
Number of steps LTrajectory lengthAutomated via NUTS (No-U-Turn Sampler)
Warmup iterationsAdapt parameters, burn-in100-500 iterations (depends on complexity)

Diagnostics (Essential!)

  • R-hat (Ř) < 1.01: Chain convergence diagnostic. Multiple chains must agree.
  • ESS (Effective Sample Size): Number of independent samples equivalent to your correlated chain. Higher is better.
  • Divergent transitions: If present, the posterior geometry is problematic — may need reparameterization or stronger mass matrix.

Practical Use in BNN Research

import pyro
import pyro.distributions as dist

# Define model
def model(x, y=None):
    w = pyro.sample('w', dist.Normal(torch.zeros(10,1),
                                         torch.ones(10,1)).to_event(2))
    b = pyro.sample('b', dist.Normal(0, 1))
    mu = x @ w + b
    with pyro.plate('data', len(x)):
        pyro.sample('obs', dist.Bernoulli(logits=mu), obs=y)

# Run HMC (NUTS)
nuts_kernel = pyro.infer.NUTS(model)
mcmc = pyro.infer.MCMC(nuts_kernel, num_samples=500, warmup_steps=200)
mcmc.run(x_train, y_train)

# Extract posterior samples
samples = mcmc.get_samples()

Limitations for Large NNs

  • HMC requires computing the gradient of the entire log-posterior per leapfrog step — O(N) cost per step, with N steps per sample.
  • For modern deep networks (millions of parameters), HMC is prohibitively expensive.
  • Stochastic gradient HMC (SGHMC) variants exist but lose the guarantees.
  • Practical recommendation: Use HMC only on small models or last layers. For full networks, use it only as a benchmark to evaluate cheaper approximations.
🔗 Connections: This tutorial is the pre-requisite for understanding why BNN inference is hard. It directly connects to Izmailov et al. (2021) which uses HMC as the gold standard to evaluate all other approximations. For practical implementation, check Pyro's NUTS or TensorFlow Probability's HMC.
