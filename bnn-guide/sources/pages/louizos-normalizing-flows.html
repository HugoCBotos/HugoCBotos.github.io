Multiplicative Normalizing Flows for BNNs

Multiplicative Normalizing Flows for Variational Bayesian Neural Networks — Louizos & Welling (ICML 2017)

📄 Paper: arXiv:1603.04779
📁 Source: 2017_Louizos_Multiplicative_Normalizing_Flows.pdf
🎯 Core Idea

Extend mean-field VI by using normalizing flows to transform a simple base distribution (e.g., Gaussian) into a richer, more expressive posterior that captures correlations between weights and non-Gaussian structure.

Why Normalizing Flows?

Mean-field VI (Bayes by Backprop) assumes a fully factorized Gaussian posterior. This is known to be:

  • Too narrow (overconfident) — the 'mode-seeking' behavior of KL[q||p].
  • Incapable of capturing weight correlations (off-diagonal structure).
  • Poor at representing heavy-tailed or multi-modal posteriors.

Normalizing flows provide a way to learn a flexible posterior by composing invertible transformations:

$$ z_0 \sim q_0(z_0) \quad\text{(simple base, e.g., }\mathcal{N}(0, I)\text{)} $$ $$ z_K = f_K \circ \cdots \circ f_1(z_0) \quad\text{(compose K invertible transforms)} $$ $$ q(z_K) = q_0(z_0) \cdot \prod_{k=1}^K \big|\det J_{f_k}^{-1}\big| $$

Louizos & Welling's Approach

The key innovation is a multiplicative parameterization of the flow specifically designed for BNN weight posteriors:

  • Global noise variables z ~ q(z) that are shared across weights, reducing the effective dimensionality.
  • Local scaling via auxiliary random variables that make the posterior flexible while keeping computation tractable.
  • The flow acts on pre-activations rather than individual weights (similar to local reparameterization in Bayes by Backprop).

Formulation

$$ w = \mu + \sigma \odot r(z), \quad\text{where } r(z) \text{ is a normalizing flow} $$
  • $r(z) = z$ — standard Bayes by Backprop (Gaussian posterior)
  • $r(z) = \text{IAF}$ — inverse autoregressive flow (richer posterior)
  • $r(z) = \text{planar flow}$ — simpler, cheaper flow

Results

  • Significantly better predictive log-likelihood than mean-field VI on UCI regression benchmarks.
  • Better-calibrated uncertainty — wider predictive intervals when data is scarce.
  • Captures weight correlations that are invisible to mean-field methods.
  • ~2-5× more expensive than Bayes by Backprop (depends on flow complexity).

Pros & Cons

✅ Advantages❌ Disadvantages
Richer, more accurate posteriors than mean-field VI Significant implementation complexity
Captures weight correlations and non-Gaussian structure Computational cost grows with flow depth
Better uncertainty calibration Flow design requires careful tuning (architecture choices)
Compatible with the reparameterization trick gradient estimator Still a variational approximation — can miss true posterior modes
🔗 Connections: This work bridges VI and normalizing flows. Modern extensions include neural spline flows, continuous flows (neural ODEs), and diffusion-based posteriors. Izmailov et al. (2021) showed that even flows may not fully capture the complexity of real BNN posteriors.
