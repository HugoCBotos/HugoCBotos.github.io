Bayesian Neural Networks Technical reference compendium — key papers, ideas, and mathematical foundations

This site collects freely available references on Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs), organized by approach. Each card links to a detailed technical page explaining the core ideas, mathematical formulation, and relevance for practical neural network development.

🔁 Taxonomy of BNN Approaches

Bayesian neural networks extend standard NNs by placing a distribution over weights rather than using point estimates. Inference is generally intractable, so approximations are needed. Here is the landscape:

VI (Bayes by Backprop) → Dropout ≈ VI → Laplace → HMC (Gold Standard) → Posterior Analysis

Each approach trades off computational cost vs. fidelity. HMC is the gold standard but impractical at scale; VI and Laplace are fast but make strong assumptions. Understanding these tradeoffs is essential for applied BNN work.

📐 1. Variational Inference for BNNs

Variational Inference (VI) approximates the true posterior p(w|D) with a simpler distribution qθ(w) by minimizing KL-divergence. This is the most popular family of methods for scaling BNNs.

Bayes by Backprop

Blundell et al., 2015 — The canonical VI method for BNNs. Uses the reparameterization trick to learn a Gaussian posterior over weights via standard backpropagation. Key innovation: local reparameterization reduces gradient variance.

variational foundational

Probabilistic Backpropagation

Hernández-Lobato & Adams, 2015 — Propagates distributions through the network using analytical moment-matching, avoiding Monte Carlo sampling entirely. Fast and deterministic.

variational

Multiplicative Normalizing Flows

Louizos & Welling, 2017 — Extends VI with structured posteriors using normalizing flows. Captures correlations between weights for richer uncertainty representation.

variational flows

🎲 2. Dropout as a Bayesian Approximation

Dropout as Bayesian Approximation

Gal & Ghahramani, 2016 — A landmark result: standard dropout training is equivalent to variational inference in a deep Gaussian process. Enables uncertainty estimates from existing deterministic NNs with near-zero extra cost at training time (though more epochs may be needed for convergence).

dropout foundational

📊 3. Laplace Approximations

Laplace Redux

Daxberger et al., 2021 — Modern take on the classic Laplace approximation. After MAP training, approximate the posterior as a Gaussian around the mode using the Hessian (or its approximations). Scalable to modern architectures via Kronecker-factor or diagonal approximations.

laplace

🎯 4. MCMC — The Gold Standard

Conceptual Introduction to HMC

Betancourt, 2017 — The definitive modern tutorial on Hamiltonian Monte Carlo. Explains the geometry of HMC, the necessity of MCMC for BNNs, and practical diagnostic tools (R-hat, ESS).

MCMC

🔬 5. Empirical Understanding of BNNs

What Are BNN Posteriors Really Like?

Izmailov et al., 2021 — Large-scale empirical study comparing VI, Laplace, HMC, and SWAG across many architectures. Key findings: posteriors are highly complex (multi-modal, heavy-tailed); common VI assumptions (Gaussian, diagonal) are badly misspecified; HMC remains the reference standard.

empirical

📖 6. Tutorials & Comprehensive Surveys

Start here if you're new to the field or want a broad landscape view.

Hands-on Bayesian Neural Networks

Jospin et al., 2022 — A practical tutorial with code examples (PyTorch, TensorFlow Probability, Pyro). Covers VI, MC Dropout, ensembles, and Laplace from an implementation perspective.

tutorial

Bayesian Neural Networks: An Introduction and Survey

Goan & Fookes, 2020 — Clear, pedagogical survey covering the full spectrum from probabilistic foundations to advanced variational methods.

survey

A Survey on Bayesian Deep Learning

Wang & Yeung, 2020 — Broad coverage of Bayesian deep learning including BNNs, Bayesian CNNs, Bayesian RNNs, and connections to deep Gaussian processes.

survey

Bayesian Learning for Neural Networks: A Survey

Magris & Iosifidis, 2023 — The most recent comprehensive survey (2023). Up-to-date taxonomy, covers modern scalable VI, last-layer methods, and connections to uncertainty quantification.

survey

⚡ Quick Comparison

Method Posterior Type Training Cost Uncertainty Quality Scalability Key Limitation
Bayes by Backprop Gaussian (diag. cov.) 2× forward pass Moderate Good Gaussian assumption too simple
Prob. Backprop Gaussian (moment-matched) ≈ 1× (deterministic) Moderate Very good Limited to certain architectures
MC Dropout Bernoulli approx. ≈ 1× (same as standard) Moderate Excellent Approximation quality debated
Laplace Gaussian (MAP + Hessian) ≈ 1× (post-hoc) Good (last-layer) Excellent Local approximation around mode
HMC Unbiased (MCMC) 100-1000× Gold standard Poor Computationally prohibitive for large nets
Normalizing Flows Expressive (flows) 2-5× Good Moderate Complex implementation, tuning
💡 Recommendation for practitioners: For large-scale deep learning, start with MC Dropout (near-zero extra training cost, though may need more epochs) or Laplace approximation (post-hoc, lightweight). For smaller models where uncertainty quality matters, consider Bayes by Backprop or Normalizing Flows. Always benchmark against HMC on a small model to understand the true posterior before committing to an approximation.
